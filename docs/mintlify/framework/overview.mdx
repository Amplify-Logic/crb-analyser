# The CRB Framework

The CRB (Cost-Risk-Benefit) framework is a structured methodology for making technology decisions. It replaces gut-feel evaluations with evidence-based scoring across three dimensions.

## Core Principle

> **The analysis must make the best option obvious.**

Every recommendation produces a single **NET Score** that accounts for costs, risks, and benefits:

```
NET SCORE = Total Benefit - Total Cost - (Total Risk / 10)
```

The highest NET score is the recommended option. No ambiguity, no hand-waving.

## Time Horizons

Every analysis covers three time horizons:

| Horizon | Period | Focus |
|---------|--------|-------|
| **Short-term** | 0-6 months | Implementation costs, quick wins, immediate risks |
| **Medium-term** | 6-18 months | Adoption trajectory, ROI timeline, emerging risks |
| **Long-term** | 18+ months | Strategic value, total cost of ownership, market evolution |

## Priority Tiers

Findings are ranked into four tiers based on their NET score and implementation complexity:

| Tier | Criteria | Action |
|------|----------|--------|
| **Quick Win** | High benefit, low cost, low risk | Implement immediately |
| **Strategic** | High benefit, moderate cost/risk | Plan and execute in phases |
| **Consider** | Moderate benefit, needs further evaluation | Evaluate when capacity allows |
| **Defer** | Low benefit or high risk | Revisit in 6-12 months |

## Confidence Levels

Every data point carries a confidence level:

| Level | Factor | Meaning |
|-------|--------|---------|
| **High** | 1.0 | Verified vendor pricing, published benchmarks |
| **Medium** | 0.85 | Industry estimates, comparable data |
| **Low** | 0.70 | Extrapolated or limited data |

Confidence factors are applied to all projections, ensuring conservative estimates when data is uncertain.
